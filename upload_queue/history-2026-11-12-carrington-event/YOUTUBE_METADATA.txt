TITLE:
You're a Telegraph Operator in 1859. Then the Sky Catches Fire.
DESCRIPTION:
Boston, the small hours of 2 September 1859. Your telegraph is throwing sparks, the sky is blood red, and the wire is talking with no one on the other end. This is the Carrington Event, the most intense geomagnetic storm in recorded history, lived through the eyes of one young night operator.

You'll see Richard Carrington catch the first solar flare ever recorded (and miss most of it by running out of the room), miners in the Rockies cooking breakfast at 1 a.m., operators getting shocked by their own lines, and the real Boston–Portland exchange where two operators unplugged their batteries and sent messages powered by the storm alone. Then: what a storm like this would do to us today.

YOU WERE THERE · Episode 2. History told through one ordinary person who lived it.

Ned Barlow is a fictional composite character. The events around him are documented: the Boston–Portland wire messages are quoted as the Boston Evening Traveler printed them in 1859; all other dialogue is dramatized. All images are animated reconstructions, not historical pictures. Figures such as the 17.6-hour travel time and the Lloyd's damage estimate are modern estimates.
