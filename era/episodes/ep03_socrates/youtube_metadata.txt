THUMBNAIL TEXT: HIS STUDENT? · ATHENS 399 BC

DESCRIPTION
What if you were born in ancient Athens, and the strange barefoot man in the market picked YOU for a conversation?

In this animated story you grow up in the potters' quarter, survive the plague, get grilled by Socrates about what makes a "good" man, watch him corner a famous general, crash a real symposium with Alcibiades, hear the story of the Oracle of Delphi, live through the Thirty Tyrants, sit in the courtroom with 501 jurors, and stand in the prison cell on his last day.

Most of the dialogue is adapted from Plato's own dialogues (Apology, Laches, Symposium, Crito, Phaedo). Socrates never wrote a single word, so everything we know comes from his students.

SOURCES & FURTHER READING
- Plato: Apology (the trial speech, the 280–220 vote, "the unexamined life"), Laches (courage), Euthyphro (piety), Symposium (Alcibiades, Potidaea), Crito (the escape offer), Phaedo (the death scene and last words).
- Xenophon: Memorabilia and Apology of Socrates.
- Thucydides, History of the Peloponnesian War 2.47–54 (the plague of Athens).
- Aristophanes, The Clouds (423 BC); the theater anecdote is a later legend told by Aelian, Varia Historia 2.13.
- Aristotle (school of), Constitution of the Athenians 63–66 (jury selection and the kleroterion).
- Diogenes Laertius, Lives of Eminent Philosophers 2 and 9.52 (Socrates; Protagoras' fees).
- M. H. Hansen, The Athenian Democracy in the Age of Demosthenes (1991) for population and citizen estimates.
- E. Wilson, The Death of Socrates (2007); R. Waterfield, Why Socrates Died (2009).

Note: dialogue is adapted and shortened for storytelling; "you" are a fictional student. Numbers marked as estimates are approximate.
